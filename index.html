Predicting Future Content Impression Decline

A leakage-safe, client-grouped machine-learning experiment using anonymized content-performance data. The goal is decision support: identifying whether historical signals contain useful information about a future ≥20% decline in impressions.

1. Research question

Can historical content-performance signals help distinguish content items that will experience a substantial future decline in impressions?

Decision supported: prioritize content items for further review based on measured historical signals. This experiment does not claim to predict Google's ranking algorithm or establish causal relationships.

5. Limitations

  • The model did not beat the majority-class baseline on accuracy.
  • The ROC-AUC indicates only limited predictive separation in this experiment.
  • Only three historical features were used in the final model.
  • Results are based on the available anonymized warehouse release and its time windows.
  • The experiment does not establish causation.
  • It does not claim to reproduce or predict Google's ranking algorithm.
  • Performance on these anonymized clients should not automatically be generalized to other datasets.

6. Ranked recommendations

  1. Use the model as an exploratory decision-support signal, not as an automated decision maker.
  2. Investigate average-position changes first. It had the highest model feature importance in this experiment.
  3. Combine impression history with position context when reviewing potentially declining content.
  4. Collect and test additional historical signals before treating the approach as production-ready.
  5. Keep client-grouped validation in future experiments to reduce the risk of client leakage.
